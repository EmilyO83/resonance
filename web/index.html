Building Orgone

Orgone is a 50/50 matrix of metal shavings and an organic binder, wrapped around a quartz crystal. The alternating organic/inorganic layers are the whole point.

NO RESIN. Resin is an insulator. It seals the piece in plastic and blocks any energy the orgone releases. It looks pretty but deadens the device. Use a natural, breathable binder instead.

What you actually need

  • Metal: copper shavings, brass, aluminium, steel, gold/bronze leaf, mixed sizes are best.
  • Organic binder (no resin): beeswax, or beeswax + a little carnauba/plant wax. Pine rosin melted into beeswax works too, but keep it breathable.
  • Central crystal: a clear quartz point (the "engine").
  • Optional accents: smaller crystals, a copper coil, a tensor ring set in the middle.
  • Mould: silicone (muffin, pyramid, sphere, dome).

The layer recipe

Base layer, metal shavings pressed into the bottom of the mould.
Binder, warm (not hot) beeswax poured over, just enough to hold the metal.
Crystal, quartz point placed centrally, point facing up/out.
Repeat, alternate metal + binder up to the top. Aim ~50/50 metal to organic.
Warm, not hot. Melt beeswax gently (double boiler, ~62–70°C, just liquid). Excess heat isn't needed and can scorch the wax. Let each layer set before the next.
1
Prep the crystal. Cleanse your quartz (running water, salt, or sound, try the Cleanse button on the Frequency tab). Set intention if that's your practice.
2
Lay the metal base. A shallow layer of mixed metal shavings across the mould floor.
3
Bind gently. Pour warm beeswax to lock the metal. Don't drown it, you want metal density.
4
Set the quartz in the centre, point outward. Add any copper coil around it now.
5
Build up in layers, alternating metal + binder, to the top. Keep it roughly half metal, half organic.
6
Cool slowly & release. Let it fully set at room temperature, then pop it from the silicone mould.
Wear gloves & a mask with metal shavings. Melt wax in a ventilated space, never left unattended.

Tensor Rings

A tensor ring is a length of copper (or gold/silver) wire cut to a sacred cubit measure and joined into a closed loop, so the two ends complete a circuit of standing energy.

NO HEAT / NO WELD. Welding or soldering the join uses heat that breaks the ring's field, it disrupts the standing wave you cut the length for. Join the ends cold: tightly twist/wrap or crimp them, so the loop is continuous without ever melting the metal.

Materials

  • Wire: bare copper is traditional, 10–14 gauge shapes easily and holds form. Silver/gold for finer pieces.
  • Tools: tape measure, round-nose & flat pliers, wire cutters, a cylindrical form (pipe/jar) to shape the circle.
  • No torch, no solder, no flame.

Cold-join method

1
Cut to length. Use the calculator above, cubit + overlap. Cut clean.
2
Form the circle. Wrap the wire around a round form so it's a smooth, even loop with no kinks.
3
Overlap the ends. Bring the two ends alongside each other. Do not heat.
4
Wrap / twist cold. Tightly wind one end around the other (or wrap both with fine binding wire) so metal touches metal firmly and the loop is electrically continuous.
5
Compress & smooth. Crimp the join snug with flat pliers. Tuck sharp ends. The ring should feel solid and springy.
6
Tune it (optional). Hold it near the speaker on the Frequency tab and run a tone through.
Why cold matters: the cut length defines a standing wave around the loop. Melting the join with heat re-crystallises the copper at that point and interrupts the continuous circuit, a cold, tight mechanical join keeps the field intact.

The join without ruining the cubit

This is the part everyone gets wrong. The cubit is the finished circumference of the closed loop, the full circle the energy travels around. Your overlap is extra wire on top of that, not part of it.

The one rule: measure and mark your exact cubit length first, join the two marks together so they meet, and let the tails past the marks be the overlap. If you instead just cut cubit + overlap and wrap from the raw ends, you shorten the working circle by the overlap and detune the ring.
Total wire cut = cubit + overlap allowance (the calculator above).
Mark the cubit, measure the exact cubit and make a fine mark at each end of that span. The distance between the two marks IS the circle.
Bring the marks to touch, form the loop so mark meets mark. The leftover past each mark is your overlap.
Wrap the tails, not the circle, the overlap wraps back over itself; the cubit span stays untouched at full length.

Four cold joins (no flame)

A
Butt + bind. Bring the two cubit marks together end-to-end so they just kiss, then wrap that meeting point tightly with a separate fine copper binding wire (26–28g). The main wire never overlaps itself, the binding holds the circle closed. Cleanest look, keeps the circumference dead-on.
B
Twist-wrap. Overlap the two tails past the marks and wind each tail tightly around the opposite wire, 4–6 turns each, like a whipping. The cubit span between the marks stays full length; only the tails coil. Strongest general join.
C
Coil-wrap band. Lay the tails alongside each other and wrap both together with binding wire into a tidy 1–2 cm coil "band." Looks like a deliberate collar. Great for hiding a bead or crystal at the join.
D
Interlock hook. On thick wire, bend a tiny hook into each end (round-nose pliers), hook them together at the marks, then crimp flat so they can't slip. No binding wire needed, good for chunky 10–12g rings.
Test the tuning: after joining, measure the closed loop's circumference with a soft tape right around the wire. It should read your cubit within a couple of millimetres. If it's short, you wrapped into the circle instead of the tails, re-open and re-seat the marks.
Never do: solder, braze, weld, or torch the join · glue or resin the ends · file the mark span shorter to "make it fit" · leave the join loose (a gap breaks the circuit as surely as heat does). Firm cold metal-to-metal contact is what keeps the loop continuous.

Make it pretty

  • Polish the copper (lemon + salt, or a cloth) to a warm shine.
  • Wrap the join neatly with matching copper binding wire for a clean "coil" look.
  • Bind a small quartz or bead at the join, cold-wrapped, never glued in resin.
  • Nest multiple cubit sizes concentrically for a layered piece.
